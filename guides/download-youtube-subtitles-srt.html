How to download YouTube subtitles as an SRT file

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Short answer: for your own videos, download the SRT from YouTube Studio. For any other video that has captions, use a subtitle extension or tool that saves the caption track as an SRT file with the original timing.

Method 1: YouTube Studio (only your own videos)

  1. Open YouTube Studio and go to Subtitles.
  2. Pick the video and the language.
  3. Open the menu next to the subtitles and choose Download, then .srt.

This only works for videos you own. It is the cleanest source because it is exactly what YouTube stores.

Method 2: a Chrome extension (any video with captions)

I make a free extension for this, YouTube Transcript & Subtitle Exporter. It adds a Transcript button next to the Subscribe button.

  1. Open the video and click Transcript. The original language is selected; you can pick any other caption track the video has, including auto-generated ones.
  2. Choose SRT (or VTT).
  3. Click the button under it. The file is named after the video and keeps the original timing, so it opens in video editors and media players.

The same popup can also save TXT (with or without timestamps) and Markdown, or copy the text. Caption export runs in your browser: the captions are not sent to any server, and it doesn't download the video or its audio.

Method 3: copy from the transcript panel (no timing file)

Under the video, expand the description and click Show transcript. You can select and copy the text, but you get timestamps as plain text, not an SRT file. Use this when you only need the words.

FAQ

Can I download subtitles from any YouTube video?

You can save captions from any public video that has them, uploaded by the creator or auto-generated by YouTube. YouTube itself only offers a download button in YouTube Studio, for videos on your own channel.

What is the difference between SRT and VTT?

Both are text subtitle files with timing. SRT is the most widely supported by video editors and media players. VTT (WebVTT) is the format used on web pages. Pick SRT unless your tool asks for VTT.

Can I download auto-generated subtitles?

Yes. Auto-generated captions are a normal caption track and can be saved as SRT or VTT like uploaded ones. Their lines are shorter, and in some languages they have no punctuation.

How do I get subtitles in another language?

Choose a different caption track if the video has one. If not, you can translate the text after exporting; the extension below can translate on your computer with Chrome's built-in translator.

Why is there no subtitle file for some videos?

Some videos have no captions at all, not even auto-generated ones. There is nothing to download then; you need speech-to-text, see the guide on videos without captions.
